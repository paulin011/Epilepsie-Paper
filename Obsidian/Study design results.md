## 1. Common pitfalls in retrospective study design (data leakage, train/test overlap)

| Source                                                                                                           | Key point                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Kalousios et al. 2024** (`ECG‐based epileptic seizure prediction Challenges of current data‐driven models.md`) | Explicitly contrasts **LOOCV** (leave-one-seizure-out) with **pseudo-prospective testing**. LOOCV "dismisses the chronological order of events" and thus allows future information to inform training; the pseudo-prospective design uses only past seizures for training, enforcing causality. They note that in pseudo-prospective testing "any potential information leakage was excluded" by nested CV and FS restricted to the training set. |
| **Wong et al. 2023** (`EEG datasets for seizure detection and prediction— A review.md`)                          | Warns that datasets from Kaggle Melbourne University's competition share patients with NeuroVista data; studies using both "need to take note to prevent data leakage producing inaccurate results."                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Beniczky et al. 2021** (ILAE guideline)                                                                        | States that **offline analysis and post-hoc cut-off selection** in phase 2 studies "raise the possibility of overfitting to the recorded dataset and question the generalizability."                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Andrade et al. 2024**                                                                                          | Highlights that when temporal seizure information is unavailable (e.g., AES/Epilepsy Ecosystem), division must ensure "no preictal files corresponding to the same seizure were present in both sets."                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

## 2. Specific examples of pseudo-prospective / leave-one-seizure-out validation

|Source|Validation approach|
|---|---|
|**Kalousios et al. 2024**|Rolling **pseudo-prospective testing**: trains on k past seizures, tests on the chronologically next seizure, repeated (n − 2) times. Illustrates difference from standard LOOCV (Figure 1).|
|**Lu et al. 2023** (`Epileptic Seizure Prediction Method Based on CBAM-3D CNN-LSTM Model.md`)|Uses **leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV)** at seizure level: one seizure is held out for testing while the remaining N−1 seizures form the training set, repeated N times per patient.|
|**Pavei et al. 2017** (`Early Seizure Detection Based on Cardiac Autonomic Regulation Dynamics.md`)|Uses LOOCV with 34 seizures: 33 preictal samples for training, 1 held out for test, repeated 34×.|
|**Lu et al. 2025** (`Leveraging Channel Coherence in Long-Term iEEG Data for Seizure Prediction.md`)|Splits data **chronologically** (first 60 % train, next 20 % validation, final 20 % test) "to prevent information leakage to the test stage."|
|**Yang et al. 2022** (`Multimodal AI System for Out-of-Distribution Generalization.md`)|Describes **pseudo-prospective** evaluation: model fixed after training on TUH training+validation, then applied without re-tuning to unseen test sets.|

## 3. Detailed protocols for prospective clinical trials (phase 3/4)

| Source                                                                                               | Protocol summary                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Beniczky et al. 2021** (ILAE/IFCN guideline)                                                       | Defines phases 0–4; **phase 3** requires: prospective, multicenter, ≥30 seizures from ≥20 patients, real-time detection with predefined algorithm/cut-off, video-EEG reference standard. **Phase 4** = in-field follow-up in patients' home environments. Reports only 3 phase-3 studies (ACC, EMG, multimodal ACC+HR) with sensitivity 90–96 %, FAR 0.2–0.67/24 h. |
| **Miron et al. 2025** (`Autonomic biosignals, seizure detection, and forecasting.md`)                | Summarizes ILAE validation standards classifying studies into phases 0–4. Describes two **phase 4** Nightwatch studies (residential adults / home-based pediatric) and a subsequent phase 3 study testing algorithm generalization from adults to children, noting mid-trial algorithm adjustment when pediatric FAR was too high (.2/h).                           |
| **Shum & Friedman 2021** (`Commercially available seizure detection devices A systematic review.md`) | References multiple phase-3 and phase-4 trials (e.g., Embrace2, Nightwatch) with reported sensitivity/FAR.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

### Suggested paragraph for subsection 2.4 (`Evaluation Frameworks`)

> Algorithm validation in seizure detection is stratified by the ILAE into phases 0–4, where phase 3 requires prospective, multicenter trials with at least 30 seizures from 20 patients, a predefined algorithm, real-time analysis, and a video-EEG reference standard \parencite{beniczkyAutomatedSeizureDetection2021a}. Retrospective designs risk data leakage when non-chronological splits (e.g., standard LOOCV) allow future information into training; robust pseudo-prospective protocols instead train on past seizures and test on chronologically subsequent events \parencite{kalousiosECGbasedEpilepticSeizure2024}. Leave-one-seizure-out cross-validation remains common but must be combined with nested feature selection and hyperparameter tuning to avoid information leakage \parencite{paveiEarlySeizureDetection2017, luEpilepticSeizurePrediction2023}. Phase 4 in-field trials—such as the Nightwatch home studies—evaluate feasibility and FAR in patients' daily environments \parencite{mironAutonomicBiosignalsSeizure2025}.
