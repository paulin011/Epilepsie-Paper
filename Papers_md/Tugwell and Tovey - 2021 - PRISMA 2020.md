# Tugwell and Tovey - 2021 - PRISMA 2020

Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 134 (2021) A5–A6 

EDITORS’ CHOICE 
PRISMA  2020 

Introduction 

A  revision  of  the  Preferred  Reporting  Items  for  Sys- 
tematic  reviews  and  Meta-Analyses  (PRISMA)  statement 
is an important event. The PRISMA 2020 statement has, 
like its predecessor (now re-referred to as PRISMA 2009), 
been published in multiple journals. This paper describes 
the changes that have been introduced in the new checklist 
[ 1 ]. However, in the June issue of JCE we also publish a 
detailed report describing the development process of the 
update. [ 2 ] 

By any measure, the papers represent a substantial body 
of  work  and  a  major  refresh  of  the  PRISMA  statement. 
The process is familiar, but the detail merits repetition –
robustness based on a broad search of the relevant scientiﬁc 
literature, focussing on those PRISMA 2009 items that are 
neglected or problematic, followed by a broad consultation 
with a wide groups of interested parties. As the authors re- 
port, over 200 methodologists and editors were invited to 
complete a survey, and half of those asked did so. This was 
followed by an in-person meeting in Edinburgh 2018 and 
further  consultations  as  the  drafting  and  revision  process 
ran  through  to  its  completion.  The  list  of  contributors  is 
impressively international and multi-professional, although 
the  leadership,  as  demonstrated  by  the  reports’  lead  au- 
thors and the principal sources of funding support, shows 
a  considerable  and  outstanding  Australian  inﬂuence.  One 
omission, acknowledged by the authors, is the absence of 
consumer or citizen involvement. 

PRISMA 2020 also represents a considerable changing 
of the guard from the personnel perspective. Some of this, 
sadly but inevitably, was unavoidable, and it is good to see 
PRISMA 2020 being dedicated to those two towering con- 
tributors to PRISMA 2009, Doug Altman and Alessandro 
Liberati. That said, the list of authors of the statement in- 
cludes many members of a new brigade of leaders within 
the international evidence synthesis community. 

Key changes

decessor highlight the changing times, both in representing 
new content that reﬂects scientiﬁc advancement, but also in 
a focus in supporting implementation. Although PRISMA

the recent iteration include several sub-items (10, 13, 16, 
20,  23  and  24).  The  changes  are  well  described  in  the 
main statement paper, but some of the changes are particu- 
larly noteworthy. One of these is an inevitable consequence 
of  the  success  of  the  original  guideline.  Since  PRISMA 
2009,  there  have  been  many  extensions  and  reﬁnements 
aimed at addressing speciﬁc areas of research activity, such 
network meta-analysis and consideration of health equity. 
PRISMA  2020  cannot  replicate  or  update  each  of  these, 
but  there  is  a  clear  intent  on  the  part  of  its  authors  to 
be more wide-ranging in its scope. Noting that PRISMA 
2009 was focussed squarely on systematic reviews of in- 
terventions, PRISMA 2020 aims to ensure relevance to a 
broader sweep of review types and research questions, in- 
cluding  mixed  methods  reviews,  those  that  evaluate  the- 
ories  of  causation,  prevalence  or  prognosis  or  those  that 
do  not  set  out  to  include  meta-analysis  or  even  synthe- 
sis. One of the reﬁnements referred to above – an updated 
version  of  PRISMA  for  Abstracts  is  folded  into  the  new 
guidelines,  which  will  be  welcomed  by  those  for  whom 
the  forest  of  reporting  guidelines  is  becoming  somewhat 
confusing. Other related guidelines are simply referenced, 
with  the  hope  that  these  will  be  updated  to  sit  comfort- 
ably  alongside  the  new  PRISMA.  The  authors  note  that 
PRISMA  2020  may  be  used  for  updated  or  ‘living’  sys- 
tematic reviews but acknowledge that additional consider- 
ations may apply in these circumstances. 

Other changes relate to amendments of the process and 
methods.  These  include  the  increasing  development  and 
use of technologies to support study selection or data col- 
lection, the assessment of risk of bias and the introduction 
of the concept of certainty to replace and expand on pre- 
vious  and  limited  measures  of  ‘study  quality’.  There  are 
also measures to address new data sources, for example tri- 
als registries, websites and repositories, and also some of 
the known challenges in existing reviews, including unac- 
knowledged changes from the protocol or registration data, 
non pre-speciﬁed sub-group or sensitivity analyses, inade- 
quately described search strategies, and so on. Those who 
worry that reviews will not only become more complicated 
to conduct but also to read, may be somewhat relieved to 
note  the  appreciation  that  some  of  the  new  elements  re- 
quired  may  reasonably  be  cited  in  an  online  supplement 
or open access repository.

PRISMA 2020 remains focussed on reporting, although 
as  the  authors  indicate,  many  elements  of  review  con- 
duct will be implied by the reporting expectations. In this, 
PRISMA is consistent with its predecessor, but differs from 
the Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention 
Reviews (MECIR), which are more extensive and address 
both conduct and reporting [3] . In a similar vein, PRISMA 
2020 explicitly avoids the temptation to stray into evalu- 
ating the overall methodological quality of the systematic 
review.  The  authors  note  that  there  are  existing  tools  to 
undertake this. [ 5 , 6 ] 

Implementation and policy 

The  science  of  implementation  has  also  developed  at 
pace  since  the  PRISMA  2009  guidelines  was  developed. 
Despite  this,  the  authors  of  PRISMA  2020  lament  the 
paucity of high certainty evidence on interventions that in- 
crease adherence to published reporting guidelines. Unde- 
terred, and with implementation in mind, the authors of the 
new reports have developed aids including a ﬁllable check- 
list and ﬂow diagram templates on the PRISMA statement 
website  (( http:// www.prisma-statement.org/ ),  and  a  web 
application to facilitate completion of the checklist (avail- 
able  at  https:// prisma.shinyapps.io/ checklist/ ).  The  check- 
list can also be exported in Word or PDF formats. 

The PRISMA 2020 authors propose actions that may be 
undertaken by journals and publishers: including ensuring 

that instructions to review authors include a strong recom- 
mendation that they use PRISMA 2020 in the preparation 
of reports prior to submission, and also that some journals 
may mandate the submission of a completed checklist with 
any appropriate systematic review. As Co-Editors of JCE, 
we commit to exploring these and further actions to sup- 
port better reporting of reviews submitted to our journal, 
in conjunction with our Editorial Board.
